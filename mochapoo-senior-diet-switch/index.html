Your Mochapoo Doesn't Need Senior Food at Seven

A vet explains when to switch your Mochapoo to a senior diet, what actually changes in the bowl, and why most senior labels mislead you.

DE
Dr. Emily Ross July 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Small poodle mixes like the Mochapoo usually shouldn't move to a mature diet until nine or ten, and the right trigger is bloodwork and body condition, not the age printed on the bag. Last October a nine-year-old Mochapoo named Biscuit came into my exam room, and her owner had proudly switched her to a senior formula at age six because the packaging said 7+. Biscuit had lost visible muscle along her spine and hips while gaining almost a pound of fat, which on a 12-pound dog is a lot. That combination, less muscle and more padding, is exactly what a badly timed senior switch produces.

Here's my position, and I'll defend it: the word senior on a dog food label is a marketing category, not a medical one. The Association of American Feed Control Officials publishes nutrient profiles for growth and adult maintenance. There is no official senior standard. So two senior foods can be nearly opposite diets, one low in protein, one high, both wearing the same gray-muzzle photo. You have to read past the front of the bag.

When a Mochapoo Actually Becomes a Senior

Small dogs age slower than big ones, which is one of the kinder facts in veterinary medicine. The 2023 AAHA Senior Care Guidelines define senior as the last 25 percent of a breed's expected lifespan. For a Mochapoo living 14 to 16 years, that math lands around age 10 or 11, not seven.

But the calendar is only half the story. I've seen this pattern over and over in practice: the body flips the senior switch before or after the birthday does. Watch for slower recovery after walks, a duller coat, stiffness on cold mornings, or drinking noticeably more water. Any of those earns a vet visit and a senior blood panel before you change a single kibble.

Run bloodwork first, then change the food. Kidney values, liver enzymes, and a urine test tell you what the diet actually needs to do. Guessing from the label is doing it backwards.

What Should Change in the Bowl (Hint: Protein Goes Up)

The biggest misconception I fight weekly is that old dogs need less protein. In most cases they need more. Aging dogs lose muscle mass faster than young ones, and dietary protein is how you slow that loss. Unless bloodwork shows real kidney disease, I want a senior Mochapoo on a diet with protein at the higher end, roughly 28 to 32 percent on a dry matter basis, from named meat sources.

Calories are the thing that should drop. A less active 10-year-old often needs 15 to 20 percent fewer of them, and the stakes are real: the Association for Pet Obesity Prevention found 59 percent of U.S. dogs overweight or obese in its 2022 survey. And lean matters more than almost anything else. The landmark 2002 Purina lifespan study in JAVMA followed 48 Labradors for life and found that dogs fed 25 percent less lived a median 1.8 years longer. Nothing in a supplement aisle comes close to that number.

Worth adding where the evidence supports it: omega-3s (EPA and DHA from fish oil) for joints and cognition, and slightly higher fiber if stools have gone unpredictable.

How to Run the Switch Without Wrecking Digestion

Go slow. A Mochapoo gut inherits the Poodle's talent for drama, and an abrupt food change is the fastest route to a 2 a.m. carpet incident. I have my clients transition over 10 to 14 days: 25 percent new food for the first three or four days, then 50, then 75, then fully switched. If stools soften at any stage, hold there for three extra days before advancing.

Full disclosure... I didn't follow my own protocol once. Years ago I switched my own dog, Waffles, cold turkey on a Friday because the old bag ran out and I figured, I'm a vet, mine will be fine. She was not fine. My friend tried the same shortcut with her Maltipoo last spring and honestly got the same result, so at least I'm in good company. I'm not 100 percent sure every dog needs the full two weeks, but I no longer gamble on which ones don't.

Weigh your dog every two weeks for the first two months after the switch. On a 12-pound dog, a half-pound drift is 4 percent of body weight. Catch it early and you just adjust portions; catch it late and you're unwinding a real problem.

Reading Past the Senior Label

Because there's no regulatory definition of senior, the burden of reading the fine print sits with you. Flip the bag over and check three things: the AAFCO statement (it should at least meet adult maintenance), the dry matter protein level, and the phosphorus content if your vet has flagged early kidney changes. Ingredient quality matters too, and regulators do pay attention to what goes in these formulas. I walked through that in my piece on what the FDA's 2021 pet food ingredient push actually meant for your dog's bowl, and the follow-up on how the FDA's 2022 warning list changed my client conversations covers the recall side.

One honest caveat: therapeutic kidney diets, which genuinely do restrict protein and phosphorus, are the exception to my protein-up rule. For a dog with diagnosed chronic kidney disease, those prescription diets have solid evidence behind them. That's a medical decision made with lab values, though, not a default setting for every gray muzzle.

And know your own dog. A food-motivated, opinionated little mix, and if yours is new to you, my guide to what first-time owners really need to know about dog temperament explains why, will happily lobby against any change. Hold the line. She'll forgive you by Thursday.

Today, do one thing: book a senior wellness panel for your Mochapoo if she's nine or older, or eight with any slowing down, and bring the food bag to the appointment. Twenty minutes of bloodwork turns the senior diet question from a guess into a plan, and it usually costs less than two months of the fancy kibble you were about to buy on instinct.

Frequently asked questions

At what age should I switch my Mochapoo to senior dog food?

For most Mochapoos, between nine and eleven years old, since small mixes often live 14 to 16 years. Base the timing on a senior blood panel and body condition rather than the 7+ printed on the bag.

Do senior dogs need less protein?

Usually the opposite. Aging dogs lose muscle, so most seniors do better on higher protein, around 28 to 32 percent dry matter. The exception is diagnosed kidney disease, where your vet may prescribe a restricted therapeutic diet.

How long should the transition to a new senior food take?

Plan on 10 to 14 days, moving from 25 to 50 to 75 to 100 percent new food in stages. If stools soften, pause at the current ratio for three extra days before increasing.
