## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the single most useful product for keeping a dog at a healthy weight?

As a veterinarian, my honest answer surprises people: a basic kitchen scale. It costs around $15, and it solves the most common cause of canine weight gain, which is inaccurate portioning.

Here's why. Most owners measure food with a scoop or cup. But scoops vary a lot, and kibble density and shape change how much fits in a level cup. I once weighed a Beagle named Pickles who was 31 pounds, and his owner insisted he got barely any food. When she showed me her scoop, it held far more than she thought. Errors of 20 to 40 percent are not unusual, and that adds up quickly over weeks and months.

Weighing the daily portion in grams removes the guesswork. Here's how I suggest using it:

1. Check the feeding guideline on your dog's food, then confirm the target amount with your vet, since it depends on age, activity, and body condition.
2. Weigh the full day's portion each morning, and subtract treats from it.
3. Track your dog's weight and body condition every two to four weeks.
4. Adjust by small increments, around 5 to 10 percent, rather than big swings.

You should be able to feel your dog's ribs under a thin layer of fat, and see a visible waist from above. If you can't, it's worth a conversation with your vet.

Other products can help too, like slow feeders, puzzle bowls, and activity trackers. They're nice extras, but none of them matter if the underlying amount of food is wrong.
