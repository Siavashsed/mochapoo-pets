## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does my Cockapoo have severe separation anxiety when other dogs don't seem to struggle as much?

This is one of the most common questions I hear in my practice, and the answer lies in genetics and neurochemistry rather than training or behavior.

Poodle mix breeds—Cockapoos, Labradoodles, Goldendoodles, and similar crosses—have a significantly higher predisposition to separation anxiety than most other dog groups. This isn't a coincidence or a training failure on your part.

Here's why: Poodles are exceptionally intelligent and form intense attachment bonds with their owners. When you cross a Poodle with another breed, you're combining that high intelligence with strong pack mentality and emotional bonding. These dogs are literally wired to need constant mental and emotional engagement. When that's suddenly removed, their brains go into a state of distress.

The destructive behavior—the baseboards being chewed, excessive barking, or house training regression—isn't stubbornness or spite. It's an anxiety response rooted in brain chemistry, not a behavior problem you can simply train away.

What actually helps is understanding this neurological component and addressing it specifically. Rather than traditional behavior modification alone, Poodle mixes respond better to anxiety-management strategies that account for their breed-specific wiring. This includes environmental management, gradual desensitization, and sometimes veterinary support.
