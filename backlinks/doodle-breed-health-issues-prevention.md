## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are doodle breeds (Goldendoodles, Bernedoodles, etc.) actually healthier than purebred dogs due to hybrid vigor?

The short answer: not automatically, and this misconception is causing real problems in veterinary practice.

Hybrid vigor is real in genetics-it describes how genetic diversity can theoretically reduce inherited disease risk. But it requires specific conditions. Doodles are first-generation crosses between two purebreds, which sounds genetically diverse on paper. In practice, though, owners and breeders aren't systematically selecting for health resilience. They're selecting for appearance and temperament.

What I see in my clinic: doodles inherit health vulnerabilities from *both* parent breeds. A Bernedoodle might carry hip dysplasia from the Bernese Mountain Dog side and sebaceous adenitis from the Poodle side. These risks don't cancel out-they compound.

Here's the dangerous part: many owners believe the hybrid advantage is automatic, so they skip preventative screenings. I've diagnosed advanced hip dysplasia, ear infections, and elbow dysplasia in doodles whose owners thought regular vet checks were unnecessary because of their mixed genetics.

The truth is that responsible health management is the same for doodles as any dog: regular screenings specific to both parent breeds, genetic testing when available, and preventative care. Hybrid vigor is a possibility, not a guarantee.
