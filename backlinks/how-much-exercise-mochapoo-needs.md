**Title:** Is the "2 hours a day" rule for doodle-type dogs actually bad advice?

I fostered a four-month-old apricot poodle mix named Biscuit back in March 2022, and I did what a lot of online guides told me to do: long, tiring outings, close to two hours a day. She got wilder, not calmer. She was overtired, she mouthed everything, and she couldn't settle in the evenings.

When I cut the total and split it into shorter chunks, she improved fast. A sniffy 15 to 20 minute walk in the morning, some training or play midday, and another easy walk at night worked much better than one huge outing.

I'm a vet with a nutrition focus, and in my experience most adult Mochapoos do well on about 45 to 60 minutes a day, split into two or three sessions. Puppies and seniors need less. A few very wired individuals need a bit more. Too much exercise, especially on young joints, can cause as many problems as too little. Mental work like scent games and short training sessions often tires them out better than another lap around the block.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How much exercise does a Mochapoo actually need each day?

Short answer: most adult Mochapoos do well on 45 to 60 minutes of exercise a day, split into two or three sessions. Puppies and seniors need less, and a few unusually wired individuals need somewhat more.

I'm a veterinarian with a focus on dog nutrition, and I disagree with the popular advice that doodle-type dogs need two hours a day. I learned this the awkward way. In March 2022 I was fostering a four-month-old apricot poodle mix named Biscuit, and I followed those long-outing recommendations. She became more hyper, mouthier, and worse at settling down. She wasn't under-exercised. She was overtired.

Here is what I'd suggest instead:

1. Split the day. A morning walk, a midday play or training session, and an evening walk work better than one marathon.

2. Match the dose to age. Puppies have growing joints and tire quickly, so keep sessions short and avoid repetitive high-impact activity like long runs or lots of jumping. Seniors do best with gentle, steady walks.

3. Add mental work. Scent games, puzzle feeders, and five-minute training sessions use up energy that physical exercise alone doesn't. Many Mochapoos are smart enough that a bored brain is the real problem.

4. Watch the dog, not the clock. If your dog settles within a few minutes after a session, the dose is about right. If they get frantic, mouthy, or can't relax, they may be overtired, and you may need less activity, not more.

5. Keep food in mind. Exercise needs and calorie intake go together. A dog on a high-calorie diet with low activity will gain weight, which puts extra strain on joints.
