## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What are the best training treats to use for puppies that won't make them overweight?

This is one of the most common questions I field in my practice, and it's great you're thinking about it early. The answer depends on balancing three factors: treat size, nutritional value, and frequency.

First, size matters tremendously. A puppy's training session shouldn't deliver massive calories. I typically recommend treats no larger than a pea for dogs under 20 pounds—this keeps each reinforcement minimal while allowing for frequent rewards that puppies need to learn.

Second, ingredient quality is non-negotiable. Many commercial training treats are predominantly fillers and preservatives with minimal protein. When you're giving 30-40 treats per session, those fillers add up fast. I've seen puppies gain unhealthy weight not from overfeeding meals, but from treat accumulation.

I worked with a case that really illustrated this: a 12-week-old golden retriever named Biscuit whose owner was using standard treats at recommended portions. When we calculated the daily calorie intake from treats alone, it was equivalent to an entire extra meal. We switched to high-protein, nutrient-dense alternatives and saw dramatic improvement in his growth trajectory and training focus.

The best options are typically: freeze-dried meat treats (pure protein, minimal processing), small pieces of cooked chicken breast (homemade option), or specialized training treats formulated to be lower-calorie and higher-nutrition than mass-market brands.
