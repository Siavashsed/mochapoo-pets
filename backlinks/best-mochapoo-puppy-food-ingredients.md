**Title:** Vet here: what I wish owners checked on a puppy food bag before paying $79 for it

I see the same pattern a lot in clinic. An owner brings in a Mochapoo puppy with loose stools and a dull, lint-like coat, and the bag at home has a wolf on the front, a premium price, and a big protein number on the label.

The front of the bag is marketing. The useful information is smaller and further back. Here's what I check first:

1. The AAFCO statement. It should say the food is complete and balanced for growth (or all life stages). Without it, you're guessing.
2. A named animal protein as the first ingredient, like chicken or salmon, not a vague 'meat meal'.
3. Moderate fat. Very rich formulas are a common cause of loose stools in small-breed puppies with sensitive guts.
4. Calcium and phosphorus in sensible ranges. Mochapoos are small-to-medium, so you don't want giant-breed numbers and you don't want an adult maintenance diet.
5. A kibble size the puppy can actually chew.

Price and a fancy label told me almost nothing about whether a food would suit the dog. The fine print told me most of it.

Also, switch foods slowly over 7 to 10 days, or you'll blame the new food for a transition problem.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the best food to feed a Mochapoo puppy?

As a veterinarian, I'd rather give you a way to judge any food than a single brand name, because the best choice depends on your individual puppy.

For a Mochapoo puppy, look for these things on the bag:

1. An AAFCO statement that the food is complete and balanced for growth (or for all life stages). This is the most important line on the label, and many owners never read it.

2. A named animal protein as the first ingredient, such as chicken, lamb, or salmon. Be cautious about vague terms like 'meat meal' or long ingredient lists that split the same ingredient into several pieces.

3. Moderate fat. Mochapoos are small-to-medium dogs, and very rich formulas are a frequent cause of loose stools and upset stomachs in puppies.

4. Appropriate calcium and phosphorus. Growing puppies need them in balance. An adult maintenance food won't provide the right levels, and a large-breed puppy formula isn't designed for a dog this size.

5. Kibble size that suits a small mouth.

I saw a good example of why this matters with an 11-week-old Mochapoo named Biscuit. Her owner had spent $79 on a large bag of premium-looking food, yet the puppy had loose stools and a dull coat. The price and packaging were persuasive. The label told a different story.

Practical tips: feed measured portions based on the bag's guidelines and your puppy's body condition, divide them into three or four meals a day, and change foods gradually over 7 to 10 days. If stools stay loose or the coat doesn't improve after a few weeks, check with your own vet, since parasites and other issues can look like a food problem.
