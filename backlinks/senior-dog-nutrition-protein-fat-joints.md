**Title:** Why do so many senior dog foods cut protein when older dogs seem to need more of it?

Something I didn't expect when I started reading senior dog food labels: a lot of senior formulas lower the protein. For most healthy older dogs, that's the wrong direction.

Dogs lose muscle as they age. This is called sarcopenia, and it starts quietly around seven. Aging dogs also use protein less efficiently, so they usually need more of it than younger adults just to keep the muscle they have. The old belief that protein damages healthy aging kidneys hasn't held up. Dogs already diagnosed with kidney disease are a different case, and their diet should come from the vet based on bloodwork.

What tends to help a healthy senior:
- Protein from named meat sources, well above the adult minimum (many senior bags sit close to it)
- A bit less fat, since most seniors move less and put on weight easily
- Omega-3s from fish oil, started before the stiffness shows, not after

An easy home check is to run your hands over the hips and spine. Bonier hips with a rounder belly usually means muscle is being lost while fat is gained.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should senior dogs eat less protein as they get older?

For most healthy senior dogs, no. They usually need more protein than they did as younger adults.

The advice to cut protein for older dogs comes from an old belief that protein strains aging kidneys. In healthy dogs, the evidence doesn't support that. What does happen with age is sarcopenia, a gradual loss of muscle that starts around seven and speeds up after that. Older dogs also use dietary protein less efficiently, so they need a higher intake just to hold on to the muscle they have. Losing muscle means weaker hindquarters, more load on aging joints and slower recovery from illness.

There is one important exception. If your dog has been diagnosed with chronic kidney disease, the protein and phosphorus targets change. That plan should be built by your vet from bloodwork, not taken from a bag label.

For a healthy senior, here is what I look for:

1. Protein from named animal sources, well above the adult minimum. Many senior formulas sit close to that minimum.
2. Slightly lower fat and calories, because most older dogs are less active and gain weight easily. Very old or underweight dogs can be the exception.
3. Omega-3s from fish oil (EPA and DHA), started early instead of waiting for stiffness.
4. Regular hands-on checks. Feel along the spine and hips. A dog can gain weight on the scale while losing muscle, so bonier hips with a rounder belly is a warning sign.

I've seen this in my own house. My beagle mix, Pickle, went on a popular 21% protein senior kibble at eight, and within a year she had visibly lost muscle over her hips.
