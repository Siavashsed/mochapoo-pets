**Title:** Why your puppy won't stop crying in the crate (and it's probably not what you think)

I just realized why crate training fails so often, and it has nothing to do with the dog being stubborn. Most people treat the crate like a switch—unfamiliar object on Friday, sleeping quarters by Saturday night. No wonder the puppy loses it.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I get my puppy to stop crying in their crate at night?

The crying typically indicates that the crate introduction happened too quickly. I see this frequently in my practice, and the solution is almost always to slow down and rebuild the relationship with the crate.

Here's what I recommend: First, stop expecting the crate to work as a sleeping space right now. Instead, treat it as a space your puppy needs to learn to love gradually. Leave the door open for several days. Toss treats inside randomly. Feed meals near or inside the crate. Let your puppy explore voluntarily without any pressure.

Once your puppy is wandering in and out freely and showing positive associations, introduce the door—open it again within seconds. This isn't about trapping your dog; it's about building positive momentum. Gradually extend the time the door stays closed while you're present and engaging with them.

The timeline matters more than most people realize. What seems like a short wait to us feels like abandonment to a young dog. Patience here actually accelerates the process because you're building genuine comfort rather than forcing compliance.

Mochapoos and similar small breeds can be sensitive during transitions, so pacing is especially important.
