The uncomfortable truth is that doodles don't escape the genetic baggage of their parent breeds. They can inherit the issues of Poodles, Retrievers and Labradors, sometimes all at once: hip dysplasia, eye disease, allergies, and chronic ear problems.

What I've learned over years of practice is that prevention beats treatment almost every time. Ask for documented health clearances on both parents, keep puppies lean during growth, and build routine checks into the first year. The cost of those habits is tiny compared with a surgical bill.

The broader lesson applies well beyond pet care: be skeptical of any claim that sounds like a guarantee, and ask what evidence sits behind it.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are doodles (Goldendoodles, Labradoodles) really healthier than purebred dogs because of hybrid vigor?

As a veterinarian and dog nutrition specialist, I get asked this a lot, and the honest answer is: not reliably.

Hybrid vigor (heterosis) is a real biological concept, but it works best when you cross genetically distant populations. Poodles, Golden Retrievers and Labradors are all modern, closed-registry breeds that share many of the same inherited weaknesses. Crossing them doesn't cancel those problems out. A doodle can inherit the risks of both sides, and occasionally all of them at once.

The most common issues I see in practice:

- Hip and elbow dysplasia, especially with Retriever or Labrador ancestry
- Eye conditions such as progressive retinal atrophy and cataracts
- Skin and food allergies
- Chronic ear infections, because the curly, dense coat traps moisture
- Obesity, which makes almost every other problem worse

I remember a 14-month-old Goldendoodle whose owners had been promised he'd be "bulletproof." His hip X-rays showed early dysplasia. It wasn't anyone's fault except a marketing myth.

The good news is that most of this is preventable or manageable:

1. Ask for OFA (or equivalent) hip and elbow results and eye clearances for both parents, and verify them yourself.
2. Keep your puppy lean. Extra weight is the biggest controllable stressor on growing joints.
3. Feed a diet appropriate for large-breed growth.
4. Avoid repetitive high-impact exercise on hard surfaces until growth plates close.
5. Check and dry the ears regularly, and groom the coat consistently.
6. Schedule regular wellness exams so problems are caught early.
