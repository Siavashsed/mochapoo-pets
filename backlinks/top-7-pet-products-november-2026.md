## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Which pet products are actually worth buying for a dog, according to a vet?

Short answer from the exam room: the products worth your money fix a problem you actually have, or make good habits easier to keep. The most overhyped ones report problems you could already see.

Here's how I'd prioritize for most dogs:

1. Parasite prevention you will actually stick to. Many owners miss monthly flea and tick doses. Vets now offer a once-a-year injectable option, and for the right dog it ends the monthly wrestling match. Ask your vet whether your dog's age, health and local parasite risk make it a good fit.

2. Quality food matched to life stage. Puppies, adults and seniors have different needs. A complete and balanced diet that meets AAFCO standards matters far more than trendy ingredients on the label.

3. Dental care. Daily brushing is the gold standard. If that won't happen, chews or water additives with the VOHC seal are a reasonable backup.

4. A properly fitted harness and ID tag. They're not exciting, but they prevent real emergencies.

Where I'd be cautious: smart collars and activity trackers. Some help in specific cases, like tracking recovery after surgery or watching a senior dog's sleep. For most healthy dogs, though, they mostly tell you what your dog is already telling you: they're restless, they're bored, they need to go out. Buy the basics first.
