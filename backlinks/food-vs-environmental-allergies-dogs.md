## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if my dog has a food allergy or an environmental allergy?

You can usually get a strong hint from the pattern of the itching. Then you confirm it with an elimination diet. No blood, saliva or hair test reliably tells the two apart, so be careful with kits that promise to.

Here's what I look for in the exam room.

Timing. Environmental allergies (atopic dermatitis) often start out seasonal and get worse in spring or fall, though they can become year round over time. Food allergies don't care about the season. A dog that itches the same in January as in July makes me think about diet.

Location. Both cause itchy paws, but environmental allergies favor the face, paws, armpits and belly. Food allergies more often involve the ears and the rear end, and ear infections that keep coming back are a big clue.

The gut. Soft stools, extra bowel movements or gassiness along with itchy skin push food higher on the list.

Age of onset. Environmental allergies usually show up between one and three years old. A brand new itch in a puppy under one, or in a senior dog, makes me consider food.

To actually prove a food allergy, we put the dog on a strict elimination diet for about eight weeks, using either a hydrolyzed protein prescription diet or a protein the dog has truly never eaten. Strict means no treats, no flavored chews or medications and no table scraps. If the itch clears up, we bring back the old food. If symptoms return within days to a couple of weeks, you have your answer.

As for at-home hair and saliva tests, they have returned positive results on fake fur and water samples. I've seen owners cut out dozens of perfectly good foods because of them.
