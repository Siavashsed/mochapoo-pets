## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Do Sphynx cats need more food than regular cats?

Yes. In most cases a Sphynx needs more calories than a coated cat of the same weight, and the reason is heat.

A cat's normal body temperature is around 101 to 102.5°F. Fur works as insulation: it traps a layer of warm air against the skin, so the body doesn't have to work as hard to stay in that range. A Sphynx has almost none of that. It loses heat to the room all the time and has to burn extra energy around the clock to make up for it. That's why many Sphynx owners notice their cat eats a lot, stays lean, and goes looking for laps, blankets and sunny windows.

I learned how much this matters the humbling way. In 2019 a client brought in a 3.6 kg Sphynx named Pickles who kept losing weight on 220 calories a day. The owner weighed every portion on a kitchen scale. The effort was fine. The standard calculation just didn't account for a cat with no coat.

What I suggest for any Sphynx owner:

1. Use the feeding chart only as a starting point. Most are designed around coated cats.
2. Weigh your cat every two to four weeks and track body condition. You should be able to feel the ribs easily but not see them.
3. Expect your cat to need more food when your home is cooler, especially in winter. A warm bed or a heated pad can reduce how much energy it burns.
4. Increase portions gradually, about 10 percent at a time, and recheck weight.
5. If your cat keeps losing weight while eating more, see your vet. Hyperthyroidism, parasites and other conditions can look like 'just a hungry Sphynx.'
