**Title:** Vet here: why do so many Mochapoo owners who brush daily still end up with matted dogs?

I see this at the clinic more than almost anything else in doodle-type coats. An owner swears they brush every day, the dog still comes in with tight mats behind the ears and in the armpits, and the skin underneath is pink and irritated.

The usual culprit is brushing technique, not effort. A lot of people run a brush over the top layer of the coat, and it glides right over the tangles sitting against the skin. Line brushing, where you part the hair and work down to the skin section by section, is the fix. A slicker brush plus a metal comb to check your work makes a big difference.

The other piece is the schedule. Most professionals run a Mochapoo on a full groom every four to six weeks, brushing about every other day, and nails roughly every three weeks. Stretching grooms to ten or twelve weeks is where the coat gets ahead of you, especially on a small dog with a soft, fine coat that tangles fast.

I don't think owners are lazy. The coat just grows faster than most people expect, and the damage hides close to the skin.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How often should a Mochapoo be professionally groomed?

As a veterinarian, my short answer is every four to six weeks. That surprises many owners, who often stretch it to eight, ten, or even twelve weeks because the dog still looks presentable. With a Mochapoo, looking fine on top and being fine underneath are two different things.

Mochapoos, a cross between a Poodle and a Cocker Spaniel, tend to inherit a soft, fine, often wavy coat that grows continuously and tangles easily. Unlike a shedding breed, the loose hair does not fall out. It stays in the coat and knits together, usually first behind the ears, under the legs, around the collar, and on the belly.

I saw this clearly in March 2022 when a 14-pound Mochapoo named Biscuit came in with golf-ball-sized mats behind both ears. Her owner brushed every day. The problem was that the brushing only skimmed the surface, and the skin underneath was pink and irritated.

Here is the routine most professionals recommend:

1. Full groom every four to six weeks. This keeps the coat at a length you can realistically maintain.
2. Brush every other day, at minimum. Use a slicker brush and a metal comb, and part the hair to work down to the skin, not just across the top.
3. Trim nails about every three weeks. Long nails change how a dog walks and can strain the joints over time.
4. Check ears regularly. Floppy, hairy ears trap moisture, so look for redness or odor.

If your dog is already matted, do not try to cut mats out with scissors at home. The skin is thin and easy to nick. A groomer can safely clip them down.
