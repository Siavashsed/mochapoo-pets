## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What dog products are actually worth buying, and how can I tell good ones from overhyped ones?

As a veterinarian focused on dog nutrition, I get this question constantly, and the honest answer is that price and packaging tell you very little. Americans spent around $152 billion on pets in 2024, according to the American Pet Products Association, so the market is crowded with products designed to catch your eye rather than serve your dog.

Here is the framework I use.

First, start with the problem, not the product. Ask what specific issue you are solving: safety, nutrition, dental health, boredom, comfort. If you cannot name it, you probably don't need the item.

Second, test for real-world performance. I learned this the hard way when I put a GPS collar on my beagle mix, Biscuit, and still lost track of him for hours. Battery life, signal coverage, and setup mattered far more than the feature list on the box. Always check how a product performs in the conditions you will actually use it in.

Third, look at ingredients and materials. For food and treats, check the first ingredients, named protein sources, and whether the company can show feeding trial or nutritional adequacy statements. For toys and gear, look at durability and whether pieces can break off and be swallowed.

Fourth, be skeptical of the shiniest option. In my own testing, the most heavily marketed product was not the winner, and a budget pick nearly made my top three. Expensive does not mean better.

Fifth, talk to your vet before changing diet or adding supplements, since needs vary by age, breed, and health history.
