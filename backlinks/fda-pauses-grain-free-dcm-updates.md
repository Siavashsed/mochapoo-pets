## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Now that the FDA has stopped updating its grain-free DCM investigation, does that mean grain-free dog food is safe?

No. That's the most common misreading of the notice, and I've been correcting it with clients all month.

The FDA said it won't post more public updates on its investigation into diet-associated dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) until there's meaningful new scientific information. That decision is about communication. It isn't a finding. The agency hasn't concluded that grain-free diets are safe, and it hasn't concluded that they cause heart disease. The question is still open.

It also helps to know what the concern actually is. Grains themselves were never the issue. The reports that started in 2018 pointed to diets high in pulses like peas, lentils and chickpeas, or potatoes, often listed among the first ingredients. Plenty of foods sold as "grain-free" fit that description, and some foods that contain grains do too. The mechanism still isn't fully understood. Taurine plays a role in some cases, but some affected dogs had normal taurine levels, so supplementing alone doesn't settle it.

Here's what I tell owners:

1. Don't panic-switch overnight. A sudden diet change brings its own problems.
2. Read the ingredient list. If legumes or potatoes dominate the top five, raise it with your vet.
3. Ask about a cardiac screen if your dog is a breed with known DCM risk, or shows tiring on walks, coughing, fainting or fast breathing at rest.
4. Choose foods from manufacturers that employ board-certified nutritionists and do feeding trials, whether or not the food contains grain.
